# 12 · MM OS — quick guide

One page. Keep it near your workstation or in your locker. If something here doesn't match
what's on your screen, ask IT — this page gets updated when the software changes.

## Signing in

You have two ways in. Most people start with the first one.

**Employee code and PIN**

1. Open MM OS in your browser (ask IT for the address if you don't have it saved).
2. Under "or employee code," type your employee code (e.g. `MM19`) and your PIN.
3. Select **Sign in**.

If you don't have a PIN yet, ask IT to issue you one. You'll be given a PIN once, on the
spot — write it down somewhere safe. IT cannot look up your PIN later; if you forget it,
they issue you a new one.

**Google**

If you have a MiniMines email address (or have already linked a personal Google account —
see below), select **Continue with Google** instead and sign in with that account.

## If your PIN doesn't work

- **"That PIN is not correct."** — you typed the wrong PIN. Try again carefully. Your PIN is
  4 to 8 digits, nothing else.
- **"No account matches that employee code."** — check you typed your employee code
  correctly. If it still doesn't work, ask IT.
- **You keep seeing "Incorrect employee code or PIN" even though you're sure the PIN is
  right** — after 5 wrong attempts in a row, your account locks itself for 15 minutes. The
  message on screen doesn't change to say "locked," so if this is your sixth attempt or
  later, **stop and wait 15 minutes** rather than continuing to try — trying more will not
  unlock it any sooner, and MM OS will not show you a different message during the wait.
- Still stuck after 15 minutes? Ask IT to issue you a fresh PIN — that also clears the lock
  right away.

## Linking a Google account (optional, do this once you have one)

If you get a MiniMines email address, or you'd simply rather sign in with a personal Google
account than remember a PIN, you can link one to your MM OS account. Once linked:

- You can sign in with either the Google account **or** your employee code and PIN — both
  keep working.
- Only you can do this — it has to be done from your own signed-in session, so nobody else
  can link a Google account to your account on your behalf, including IT.

Ask IT for the current link address if you don't see a "Link Google account" option on your
Profile page yet — this option is being added to the Profile page and may not have arrived
on your version yet.

## What the tiles are

After you sign in, you'll see a row of tiles — one for each service you've been given access
to. Not everyone sees the same tiles: you only see the ones you actually need for your job.
If you think you're missing one, see "Something looks unavailable" below.

Selecting a tile opens that service. You don't need to sign in again once you're inside it —
MM OS already told it who you are.

## Getting back to MM OS from inside a service

Every service has a thin dark bar across the very top of the page with **MM OS** written on
the left. Select it to come straight back to your tiles. The same bar also has a **Switch
service** button (or press Ctrl+K, or Cmd+K on a Mac) if you want to jump to a different
service without going back first.

## Something looks unavailable — is it broken, or do I just not have access?

- If a tile is simply **not there** on your home screen, that's access, not a fault — you
  haven't been granted that service. Raise a request (see below) if you think you should
  have it.
- If a tile **is** there but selecting it fails, or the service opens and then errors, or a
  service someone else says is working shows you an error — that's a fault. Raise a support
  request.
- If MM OS itself won't load at all for anyone in the room, that's likely a wider outage —
  tell IT directly (phone or in person) rather than waiting on a ticket, since MM OS itself
  might be what's down.

## Raising a Service Desk request

1. Open the **Service Desk** tile from your home screen.
2. Select **New request**.
3. Choose the type:
   - **Support** — something is broken, or you need help.
   - **Automation** — you want something built or automated for you.
4. Give it a short title and describe what's wrong or what you need.
5. Set a priority (Normal is fine for most things — use High or Urgent only if it's actually
   stopping your work right now).
6. If the request is sensitive and you don't want your department to see it, tick
   **Private** — only you, whoever picks it up, and your approver (for automation requests)
   will be able to read it.
7. Submit. You'll see it appear under **My requests**, and you'll get an email when it moves
   forward.

## Requesting an automation

Use the same **New request** form, choose **Automation**, and explain what you want done and
why. IT will review it, may come back with a proposal (what it will take to build), and — for
anything that needs sign-off — your approver will need to approve it before work starts. You
can follow the whole thing under **My requests**; nothing skips a step silently.

## Who to call

- **Can't sign in, locked out, need a PIN** — IT.
- **A service is broken for you specifically** — raise a Support request in Service Desk
  (see above), or tell IT directly if Service Desk itself won't open.
- **MM OS itself is down for everyone** — tell IT directly, don't wait on a ticket.
